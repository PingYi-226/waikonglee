Research

Cryptographic engineering, GPU computing and efficient hardware, from algorithms down to silicon.

Areas

  1. 01

    Post-quantum cryptography

    High-throughput and area-efficient implementations of the schemes that will replace RSA and elliptic curves: Kyber, Saber and FrodoKEM key encapsulation, the Falcon, Mitaka, Rainbow and MQ-based signatures, and code-based key encapsulation, on GPUs, FPGAs and embedded processors.

    Papers in this area
  2. 02

    High-speed symmetric and lightweight cryptography

    Bit-sliced and table-based AES, lightweight authenticated encryption and hash functions, block ciphers and big-integer arithmetic, pushed to record throughput on GPUs and tuned for ARM, RISC-V, AVR and FPGA targets.

    Papers in this area
  3. 03

    Privacy-preserving computation

    GPU-accelerated functional encryption for privacy-preserving support vector machines and neural-network inference, and fast number-theoretic transforms for fully homomorphic encryption.

    Papers in this area
  4. 04

    Deep-learning hardware and efficient models

    FPGA accelerators and co-processors for convolutional networks, sub-byte and power-of-two quantisation, and compact models for sensing, vision and text, many of them aimed at resource-constrained devices.

    Papers in this area
  5. 05

    Embedded processors and IoT systems

    The RISC32 family of low-power FPGA-based sensor-node processors, energy harvesting for wireless sensor nodes, and reliable IoT architectures for manufacturing, indoor positioning and remote monitoring.

    Papers in this area
  6. 06

    GPU numerical computing

    Parallel sparse LU factorisation and vector fitting on GPUs for fast circuit simulation and macromodelling, in collaboration with Carleton University.

    Papers in this area
  7. 07

    Applied security and quantum cryptanalysis

    Blockchain-based and searchable encryption systems for health and IoT data, chaos-based image encryption, new signature and public-key constructions, and quantum circuits for evaluating Grover attacks on block ciphers.

    Papers in this area

DPCrypto repurposes the GPU dot-product instructions designed for machine learning to accelerate lattice-based key exchange, and delivered the first Saber implementation on GPUs.

Open-source software

Post-quantum signatures on GPUs

  • mayo-gpu Constant-time CUDA signing and verification for the MAYO signature scheme, in coarse- and fine-grained variants plus a Tensor-Core path.
  • MLDSA-GPU-keygen-sign ML-DSA (FIPS 204) signing, verification and key generation on GPUs.
  • qruov-gpu GPU implementation of the QR-UOV post-quantum signature scheme.
  • Falcon-Mitaka Falcon-512 and Mitaka-512 lattice signatures on GPUs. High Throughput Lattice-Based Signatures on GPUs (IEEE TPDS 2024)

Tensor-Core and dot-product cryptography

  • TensorKyber The ML-KEM (Kyber) number-theoretic transform reformulated as integer matrix multiplications on Tensor Cores.
  • tensorMcEliece Batched Tensor-Core engines for the Classic McEliece key encapsulation mechanism.
  • Tensorcrypto Tensor-Core polynomial convolution for NTRU, LAC and FrodoKEM. TensorCrypto (IEEE Access 2022)
  • DPCrypto FrodoKEM and Saber accelerated with GPU dot-product instructions. DPCrypto (IEEE TCAS-I 2022)

Homomorphic encryption

  • cuTraNTT Number-theoretic transform kernels for CKKS homomorphic encryption on GPUs.

Symmetric and lightweight cryptography on GPUs

  • AES-BS-GPU Bit-sliced AES-128-CTR on GPUs.
  • lwcnist-finalists GPU implementations of finalist hash functions from the NIST lightweight cryptography standardisation. Efficient Implementation of Lightweight Hash Functions on GPU and Quantum Computers (IEEE Access 2022)
  • ntt-gpu-qTESLA Nussbaumer algorithm and number-theoretic transform on GPUs, applied to qTESLA. Parallel implementation of Nussbaumer algorithm and number theoretic transform on a GPU platform (J. Supercomput. 2021)
